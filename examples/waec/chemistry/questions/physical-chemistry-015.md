  During the electrolysis of an aqueous solution of copper(II) sulphate, both Cu²⁺ ions and H⁺ ions (from water dissociation) migrate to the cathode. However, copper is less reactive than hydrogen and is lower in the electrochemical series. Therefore, Cu²⁺ ions are preferentially discharged over H⁺ ions, leading to the deposition of copper metal at the cathode.
