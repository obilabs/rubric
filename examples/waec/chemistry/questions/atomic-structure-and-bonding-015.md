explanation: |
  Copper is a metal, and its atoms are held together by metallic bonding. In metallic bonding, the valence electrons are not localized to individual atoms but are delocalized and free to move throughout the entire metallic structure, forming a "sea" of electrons. These mobile electrons are responsible for copper's excellent electrical conductivity.
